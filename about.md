## Background

I am a PhD candidate in Civil Engineering at Tianjin University, working in earthquake engineering with a focus on soil–foundation–structure interaction and strong ground motion. My doctoral research is advised by Prof. Maria I. Todorovska at Tianjin University and Prof. Mihailo D. Trifunac at the University of Southern California.

My work combines numerical simulation, seismic wave propagation, strong-motion data analysis, and field measurements. Alongside my doctoral research, I participate in structural health monitoring and field-testing projects involving buildings, bridges, seismic sensor networks, and vibration measurements.

## Current work

My current research focuses on the nonlinear response of soil–foundation–structure systems subjected to large near-fault ground motions. I use finite-element and finite-difference methods to investigate the effects of soil nonlinearity, foundation flexibility, wave incidence, and near-fault pulse-like motions on structural response.

I am also developing computational tools for generating seismic input motions, including plane waves, surface waves, and near-field motions for numerical models. In parallel, I work on strong-motion databases and ground-motion prediction equations, with current studies involving seismic records from Iran and the Yunnan region of China.

My experimental work includes structural health monitoring, management and analysis of continuous seismic records, field measurements of buildings and ground vibrations, and maintenance of seismic instrumentation and monitoring systems.
