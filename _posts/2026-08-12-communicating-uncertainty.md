Uncertainty communication is not a final styling decision. The form of the result determines which uncertainty a reader notices, which they ignore, and which comparisons they can make.

## Show the reference point

An interval without a visible reference can be technically correct but hard to interpret. Pair ranges with a baseline, target, or previous estimate when that comparison matters.

## Separate variability from confidence

Observed spread and uncertainty in an estimate answer different questions. Label them directly, and avoid relying on color alone to distinguish them.

## Write the limitation next to the claim

Do not make readers search for the caveat. Place the boundary of a claim in the same paragraph, figure caption, or interaction as the result it qualifies.

These principles are simple, but applying them consistently changes how confidently a technical result can be used.
