It is tempting to frame a project around the quantity a model predicts. A stronger starting point is often the decision someone will make after seeing that prediction.

## Ask what changes

If the estimate moves, what action changes? If no action changes, greater predictive precision may not create practical value.

## Measure the cost of being wrong

False positives and false negatives rarely have equal consequences. Evaluation should reflect the decision context rather than only a generic accuracy metric.

## Keep the explanation close

A result is most useful when a reader can inspect its important evidence, assumptions, and sensitivity without leaving the task at hand.

That decision-first perspective often produces a smaller, clearer, and more credible research system.
